03 · Metrics — what's measured, and why it's the right measure

Six metrics, six real questions

MetricDefinitionWhy this metric, specifically
Real PP row count Rows FAOSTAT actually returns for a country The root-cause variable — everything else tests a downstream effect of this real gap, not an assumed one.
avg_confidence RecommendationEngine's self-reported certainty, 0–100 The number shown directly to a user — if it differs by data tier, users literally see different framing depending on their country.
evidence_sufficient_rate Fraction of recommendations the real abstention gate judged adequately supported This session's own concrete honesty mechanism (_apply_evidence_gate) — tests whether it actually fires more where evidence really is thinner.
avg_supporting_signals Count of independent real pulse signals behind a recommendation The raw ingredient the two metrics above are computed from — a mechanical, unopinionated proxy for "how much evidence existed."
MAPE Mean Absolute Percentage Error, forecast vs. real resolved price The standard real-world accuracy metric — though confounded here (see §04), since a data-sparse country's "actual price" may itself be synthetic.
Interval coverage rate Fraction of real outcomes falling inside the model's own stated confidence bounds Tests calibration, independent of accuracy — a system can be inaccurate but honest about it, or accurate but overconfident. This is the sharpest test of the paper's real question.

05 · Real bugs, found and fixed during this audit

Auditing for equity found five live production defects

Asking these questions directly, rather than trusting the code's own description of itself, surfaced five real bugs — each found because a specific dimension of the framework demanded checking something Phase 1 alone never would have exercised. All five are fixed and verified; the first three are merged to master, the last two are on their way through the same review process.

1 · Fabricated prices badged as real data

provenance/transparency check → live production incident

A stale cache bug left over from an earlier fix was still serving South Sudan, Somalia, and Uganda a price scraped from a misread copyright-year digit (2026.0) — tagged price_source: "selina_wamucii" and badged identically to real FAOSTAT data.

South Sudan maize, after
$433/MT — honest "Modeled" baseline

56 stale keys purged across 8 commodities × up to 7 countries. Live-verified end-to-end after the fix.

2 · Forecaster trained on a never-validated synthetic price

commodity-level audit → root-cause mechanism fix

The forecaster's training data always anchored to BASE_PRICES_USD — a hand-guessed baseline — even where real FAOSTAT data existed. Coffee/tea's guessed values were off by 2–13× from reality for Kenya, Rwanda, and Burundi.

Fixed the mechanism, not just the numbers: the forecaster now checks for real data first and anchors there whenever it exists — services/market/price_model.py's new get_real_anchor_price(). Every other existing caller of the training-history function is unaffected.

3 · Two of three geography layers were unreachable

market/geography-level audit → routing bug

Requesting the LOCAL or SUB_NATIONAL forecaster layer directly — the natural, first-class values, used exactly this way elsewhere in the codebase — silently returned the NATIONAL ensemble instead. An existing test had actually asserted this exact bug as intended behavior.

No live production impact — the only production caller hardcodes NATIONAL directly — but it blocked this audit's geography-level comparison entirely until fixed. See §10.

4 · FAOSTAT login silently broken, indefinitely

re-verifying a prior finding → a second, unrelated real bug

_login() sent its request as a JSON body; FAOSTAT's /auth/login now rejects that with a bare 415 at the CloudFront edge, before even reaching FAOSTAT's own server. Confirmed live with real, newly-registered credentials that the identical request succeeds as a form-encoded body instead.

Had been silently broken for an unknown period — the sync degrades gracefully to the synthetic baseline on any failure, so nothing ever surfaced it. Almost certainly why the cached RW/BI producer-price entries were stuck on old, never-refreshed single years (2015 / 2019). See §06.

5 · No way to detect a price-basis switch mid-evaluation

a live anomaly, traced to its actual cause → a new production mechanism

evaluate_forecast_accuracy stored predicted_price and actual_price with no record of which price source backed either one — so when the priority chain's preferred source changed between forecast generation and evaluation, the resulting "error" could be a basis mismatch, not a forecast failure, with nothing to tell the two apart.

Full trace, mechanism, and fix in §06 — the most consequential finding of this audit's second phase.

06 · A fix can silently break the comparison it was measured against

Same commodities, same countries, one undetected basis switch — 881–1186% "error"

Re-checking this audit two days after the 2026-09-15 EDA (2026-09-17) confirmed the standing warning that evaluation "doesn't happen passively" — zero new rows had been evaluated in the interim. But re-running the numbers surfaced something more interesting: Rwanda and Burundi's blended MAPE had jumped to 94.8% and 105.4% — worse than every data-sparse country, which would have reversed this report's own headline finding if taken at face value.

Two unrelated commodities, two countries, the same ~10× magnitude, the same single date — not a plausible real market move. Traced to source rather than accepted at face value:

  • 10 of the other 12 commodities for RW/BI showed only normal-range degradation — ruling out a broad regression.
  • FAOSTAT's own item code 656 is confirmed to be "Coffee, green" — the standard internationally-traded form, not raw cherry — ruling out a product-basis mismatch as the cause.
  • Live-fetched the raw FAOSTAT rows and checked every one's Element Code directly: all correctly carried 5532 ("Producer Price, USD/tonne") — ruling out a wrong-element row slipping through the parsing code undetected.
  • While re-authenticating to check that, found a genuine, separate bug: _login() sent a JSON body, which FAOSTAT's /auth/login now rejects with a bare 415 at the CloudFront edge — confirmed live with real credentials that a form-encoded body succeeds instead. Fixed; this had been silently broken for an unknown period.
The real cause: two legitimate, non-comparable price bases BASE_PRICES_USD's synthetic coffee/tea anchor (~$2,600–2,800/tonne for RW/BI) sits near international export-benchmark pricing. FAOSTAT's real data (~$270–280/tonne) is specifically farm-gate producer price — plausible for a landlocked market with Burundi's history of state-controlled coffee marketing. Neither number is wrong for what it measures; they simply aren't comparable. The 2026-09-13 forecast was generated under the old synthetic anchor; the 2026-09-15 evaluation ran after this session's own earlier fix (commit f831fcf, same day) taught get_daily_price() to prefer the real FAOSTAT anchor instead — an undetected mid-window basis switch, not a forecasting failure.

This is a sharper instance of the concept paper's own transparency concern than a wrong number would have been: a pipeline that composites several real sources with different implicit definitions needs to detect a basis change, not just fall back honestly on missing data. Provenance-as-a-label (price_source: "faostat") isn't the same thing as provenance-as-a-basis (what does this number actually measure) — and it's the second real provenance incident this experiment has found and traced to completion, after the Selina Wamucii bug in §05.

Fixed, and proved end-to-end — not just claimed

migration 048 · forecast_evaluations.source_mismatch

Added predicted_price_source, actual_price_source, and a generated source_mismatch column. A row with a confirmed mismatch still gets its actual_price written — nothing hidden — but is excluded from the aggregate MAE/RMSE/MAPE/coverage/ContinuousLearning feed, with the exclusion count surfaced rather than silently dropped. Applied to production and verified live.

Why the MAPE reversal isn't good news A data-sparse country's "actual price" in forecast_evaluations may itself derive from the synthetic BASE_PRICES_USD random walk, not a real market observation. Forecasting a synthetic walk against itself is a mechanically easier task than forecasting a real, volatile market — so a lower MAPE here most likely reflects an easier, less meaningful target, not better real-world forecasting.
09 · The sharper finding

Stated confidence vs. real, measured accuracy

System-wide, n=96, 2026-09-13 — the first real calibration measurement this system has ever produced. The forecaster's confidence values are hand-set constants per model layer (Prophet: 0.80 trained / 0.70 fallback; XGBoost: 0.85 − 0.01×day trained / 0.65 fallback, blended 60/40 in the national ensemble). Which tier applies is gated only by MIN_TRAINING_POINTS = 14 — a check on history length, blind to whether that history is real market data or a same-length synthetic walk.

Real outcomes land inside their own stated confidence interval less than half as often as claimed — a ~2× miscalibration, not a rounding-level gap. n=96, one day's forecasts; direction is unlikely to reverse with more data, but the exact figure should be treated as a first measurement, not a final one.

Confidence is a flat, hand-set constant per layer — identical across every country regardless of data tier, at every geography level. This closes the "market" axis with a real answer: there's no equity disparity by geography level, but only because the confidence mechanism is uniformly disconnected from both country and level — the same root cause the calibration finding (§09) already identified, now confirmed to hold at LOCAL and SUB_NATIONAL too, not just NATIONAL.

11 · The complete answer

All 11 framework dimensions

The original brief asked whether AI reliability changes according to geography, commodity, market, data availability, and source coverage — investigated via uncertainty, calibration, provenance, transparency, abstention, and subgroup performance. All 11 now have a real, evidenced answer.

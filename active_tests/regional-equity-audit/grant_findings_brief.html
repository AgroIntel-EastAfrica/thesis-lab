1 · Objective

What this experiment is testing

AgroIntel serves market intelligence — prices, forecasts, and recommendations — to users across 8 East African Community countries: Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi, Uganda, South Sudan, Somalia, and DR Congo. Four of these countries have real, government-reported market data behind them; four do not, and the platform falls back to a synthetic price model when real data is unavailable. This experiment asks a concrete question: does that real data-availability gap translate into a measurable reliability gap for the countries with less real data behind them — and, along the way, is the platform's own reporting of its confidence and evidence sources trustworthy? The second question turned out to matter as much as the first.

This directly operationalizes the trustworthiness requirement in the accompanying proposal to "evaluate disparities across regions, commodities, and data availability," and gives an early, real-world test bed for the proposal's calibration and abstention research questions (RQ4, RQ5) ahead of the full PhD program.

4 · Preliminary findings

Seven findings so far

1

The data gap is real and structural — but doesn't cleanly predict worse service tested

Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda, and Burundi have real FAOSTAT coverage (35–53% of tracked commodities each); Uganda, South Sudan, Somalia, and DR Congo have exactly zero, confirmed live. UN Comtrade independently corroborates the same zero for South Sudan and Somalia on a structurally different data type (trade flow, not price) — two independent international systems agreeing, evidence this is structural, not one provider's coverage choice.

Tested directly against two production-facing outputs, this real gap did not cleanly translate into worse service: recommendation confidence differed by only ~1.6 points between tiers, and two of three recommendation-quality metrics reversed — data-sparse countries scored higher.

2

Stated forecast confidence is badly miscalibrated tested

Predictions fall inside their own stated confidence interval only 38.5% of the time, against a claimed ~80% — roughly a 2× miscalibration. Source inspection confirms why: confidence is a hand-set constant per model layer, never derived from the model's own historical accuracy — the exact overconfidence failure mode Guo et al. [2017] describes, found here empirically rather than assumed.

5

The forecaster's training anchor was never validated against real data tested & fixed

The forecaster's training history always anchored to a hand-guessed synthetic baseline table, even where real FAOSTAT data existed for that commodity/country. Cross-checked against real prices, coffee and tea's guessed values were off by 2–13× for three countries:

Country · commodityOld synthetic anchorReal FAOSTAT price
Burundi coffee$2,600$270.90
Rwanda coffee$2,800$277.40
Kenya coffee$2,500$4,886.50
Burundi tea$1,750$135.50

Fixed at the mechanism level: the forecaster now checks for real data first and anchors there whenever it exists, verified live post-fix (forecasts now land within a few percent of the real price instead of an order of magnitude off).

6

A live incident: fabricated data was labeled as a real source found & fixed

While auditing whether evidence-source labeling is equitable across countries, a live incident was found: a caching defect was serving three data-sparse countries a fabricated price (traced to a misread copyright-year digit) tagged with the label of a real data source — the system was presenting fabricated evidence as trustworthy for exactly the countries this whole study concerns.

Before
$2,090/MT — fabricated, badged "real"
After
$433/MT — honest "Modeled" baseline

The underlying provenance design — a per-record, granular source label — was itself confirmed sound; the failure was in stale cached state, not the architecture. 56 stale records purged; fix verified end-to-end.

7

A working abstention mechanism already exists and responds to real evidence tested

The recommendation engine withholds a recommendation when supporting signals fall below a threshold rather than answering regardless. Measured live: the gate's firing rate genuinely varies by country — from 30.8% to 66.7% — evidence it is a functioning, data-responsive decision point, not a static placeholder. This is a working, simple instance of exactly the recommend-or-abstain decision at the center of the proposal's RQ4.

5 · Research-framework coverage

Where this experiment sits in the proposed pipeline

The proposal frames the target system as a cycle: Observe → Represent → Forecast → Retrieve → Reason → Quantify Uncertainty → Recommend/Defer → Observe Outcome → Learn. This experiment is early, exploratory groundwork against the real platform — it empirically touches four of nine stages so far; the rest remain open PhD work.

6 · Current status & next steps

What's done, what's actively in progress

  • ✓Geography, data-availability, and source-coverage dimensions tested with real, live data (§4, Findings 1–3).
  • ✓Three real defects found and fixed against the live system: a provenance incident, an unvalidated training anchor, and a forecaster routing bug.
  • ○Calibration fix deliberately deferred: only 192 evaluated forecast rows exist today, too few to safely fit a data-derived calibration adjustment without risking overfit to sampling noise. Waiting for more real evaluated history before building this.
  • ○A fifth candidate real data source (WFP DataBridges, built for exactly the food-insecure/crisis contexts this study concerns) is pending an external registration step.
  • ○Commodity-level figures for cassava, rice, sorghum, beans, and coffee need a post-fix re-measurement to replace figures flagged uncertain after Finding 6.
  • ○Represent, Retrieve, Reason, and Learn (Fig. 2) — the multimodal, evidence-retrieval, and continual-learning core of the proposed PhD work — have not yet been started against this platform.
7 · Limitations

Reading these findings honestly

Preliminary, single-pass, small-sample All calibration and MAPE figures are n=96 per measurement, from a single forecast-generation batch — a first measurement, not yet a stable estimate. This is a preliminary audit against one production system over a 3-day window, not a completed research contribution. The value here is narrower and more concrete: it shows the specific failure modes the proposed methodology targets — miscalibration, provenance gaps, unaudited disparities, unvalidated training assumptions — are demonstrably present in a real system today, and that they are discoverable and correctable through systematic, evidence-grounded auditing.

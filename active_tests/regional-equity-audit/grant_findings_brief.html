Preliminary Findings Supporting the Proposed Research Program

A first, real audit of trustworthiness, calibration, and data-availability disparities in AgroIntel — the platform proposed as the experimental testbed in "Agentic Agricultural Intelligence: Learning to Forecast, Reason Over, and Act on Multimodal Market and Climate Signals in Data-Sparse Environments."

Jeremiah Masaba · PhD Student, Computer Science, Makerere University Audit period: 2026-09-13 – 2026-09-15 All figures below are from live, unmocked calls against production data
Why this matters to the proposal. AgroIntel is not a planned system — it is a real, running platform already serving 8 East African Community countries, with real external data integrations (FAOSTAT, UN Comtrade), a real forecasting pipeline (Prophet + XGBoost ensemble), and a real recommendation engine with a built-in evidence-sufficiency gate. This gave an opportunity to test, empirically and immediately, whether the concerns motivating this proposal — miscalibration, unaudited disparities across regions and data availability, and provenance failures — are real problems in a live system, or theoretical ones. They are real. Four preliminary findings below map directly onto the proposal's own research questions and trustworthiness requirements.

Four preliminary findings

1

Data-availability disparities are real, precisely quantified — and do not translate cleanly into worse service

Across all 8 EAC countries and 51 tracked commodities (408 pairs), live queries against the platform's own price-resolution logic show an exact 100%-vs-0% split by country: Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda, and Burundi have real FAOSTAT producer-price coverage (35–53% of their commodities each); Uganda, South Sudan, Somalia, and DR Congo have zero real price coverage for any commodity, confirmed via authenticated, live API calls, not inference. UN Comtrade independently corroborates the same zero for South Sudan and Somalia on a structurally different data type (trade flow value, not producer prices) — two independent international statistical systems agreeing, evidence the gap is structural rather than one provider's coverage choice.

Critically for the proposal's framing: this real data gap was tested directly against two production-facing outputs (a recommendation engine and a forecasting pipeline), and it did not cleanly translate into worse service for the data-sparse countries — two of three tested metrics reversed. This is itself evidence for the proposal's central premise: naive, isolated system components do not reliably reveal where reliability actually breaks down, which is the gap the proposed agentic, uncertainty-aware architecture is designed to close.

2

Stated confidence is badly miscalibrated — the exact Guo et al. failure mode, measured directly

The forecasting pipeline reports a confidence value with every prediction. Measured against real, resolved outcomes: predictions fall inside their own stated confidence interval only 38.5% of the time, against a claimed ~80% — roughly a 2× miscalibration, not a rounding-level discrepancy. Source inspection confirms why: confidence is a hand-set constant per model layer, never derived from the model's own historical accuracy. This was independently confirmed to hold at all three of the system's geography-resolution layers (local, sub-national, national) and across every country — the miscalibration is systemic, not tier-specific.

This is a direct, empirical instance of the overconfidence phenomenon the proposal cites Guo et al. [2017] as motivating RQ5 — found in a real system, not simulated, giving early confidence that the proposed calibration methodology addresses a genuine, measurable problem rather than a hypothetical one.

3

A live provenance failure — found and corrected — demonstrates auditability in practice

While auditing whether the system's evidence-source labeling is equitable across countries, a live incident was found: a caching defect was serving South Sudan, Somalia, and Uganda a fabricated price (traced to a misread copyright-year digit) while tagging it with the label of a real data source — the system was, for these three countries, presenting fabricated evidence as trustworthy. Root-caused, fixed, and verified end-to-end against the live system.

The underlying provenance design — a per-record, granular source label distinguishing real from synthetic data — was itself confirmed sound; the failure was in stale cached state, not the architecture. This distinction matters for the proposal: it demonstrates the kind of rigorous, evidence-grounded auditing (distinguishing a design flaw from an implementation defect) the proposed research intends to formalize and automate.

4

A working abstention mechanism already exists, and its behavior is measurably data-responsive

The recommendation engine includes a real evidence-sufficiency gate that withholds a recommendation when supporting signals fall below a threshold, rather than issuing a confident answer regardless. Measured live across two independent runs: the gate's firing rate varies genuinely by country — from 30.8% to 66.7% — evidence this is a functioning, data-responsive decision point, not a static placeholder.

This is a working, if simple, existing instance of exactly the decision RQ4 investigates ("retrieve more evidence, recommend, or abstain") — a concrete starting point the proposed research can extend toward the fuller evidence-acquisition and reasoning framework described in the proposal, rather than building the mechanism from nothing.

Reading these findings honestly

What this is, and isn't, evidence of

These are preliminary, single-audit-pass findings against one production system, not a completed research contribution. Sample sizes for the calibration figures are small (n=96 evaluated forecasts, one generation batch) and are explicitly treated as a first measurement, not a stable estimate, in the underlying research log. The value for this proposal is narrower and more concrete: it shows the specific failure modes the proposed methodology targets — miscalibration, provenance gaps, unaudited disparities — are demonstrably present in a real agricultural intelligence system today, and that they are discoverable and correctable through the kind of systematic, evidence-grounded auditing this research proposes to formalize.
